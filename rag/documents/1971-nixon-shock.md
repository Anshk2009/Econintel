On 15 August 1971, President Nixon ended the US dollar's convertibility into gold, effectively collapsing the Bretton Woods system of fixed exchange rates.

Key facts (verified):
- Under Bretton Woods (mid-1940s), the US pegged the dollar to gold at $35/oz and other currencies pegged to the dollar.
- Accelerating US inflation (roughly tripling to about 6% after 1965) and persistent gold outflows undermined confidence in the dollar's fixed value.
- On 11 August 1971 Britain requested about $3bn of gold be moved from Fort Knox, accelerating the crisis.
- Nixon's 15 August 1971 package suspended gold convertibility and imposed temporary wage/price controls. The gold window never reopened; by 1973 major currencies floated freely.

Why it matters as an analogue: the origin of the modern fiat, floating-exchange-rate system — useful when analysing currency regimes, the dollar's reserve role, and a loss of confidence in a monetary anchor.
