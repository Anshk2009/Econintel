The 2007–2008 global financial crisis was the most severe financial crisis since the Great Depression. It began in the US subprime mortgage market and escalated into a full-blown international banking crisis.

Key timeline (verified):
- 17 March 2008: The Federal Reserve backstopped Bear Stearns' bad loans to enable its acquisition by JPMorgan Chase.
- 7 September 2008: The US government placed Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac into conservatorship.
- 15 September 2008: Lehman Brothers filed for bankruptcy after the Fed declined to guarantee its loans; the Dow Jones fell 504 points, its worst drop in seven years.
- 16 September 2008: The Federal Reserve Bank of New York extended an USD 85 billion loan to insurer AIG, whose credit-default-swap exposures exceeded its assets.
- 3 October 2008: The Troubled Asset Relief Program (TARP) authorised government purchases of toxic assets and equity injections into US banks.

Why it matters as an analogue: the textbook case of how opaque leverage, mispriced mortgage risk, and interconnected balance sheets turn a sectoral shock into systemic contagion — and how central-bank and fiscal backstops are deployed. Useful for analysing banking stress, contagion, and bailouts.
