How sources are balanced

Institutional primary sources come first — IMF, World Bank, OECD, and central-bank statements. Reporting is read from three different vantage points (BBC, The Hindu, Al Jazeera) so a single editorial framing doesn't become the answer. Where sources disagree, you're told they disagree rather than handed the average.

It runs on language models, so that's a fair question. The difference is constraint. EconIntel is scoped to economics and geopolitics, forced into a fixed shape — claim, mechanism, second-order effect, historical precedent — and grounded in a curated library of primary sources rather than open-ended recall. It's also instructed to say "I don't have current data on this" instead of inventing a number. A general chatbot will happily give you a confident paragraph about anything; this is built to be narrow and to show its reasoning.
AI models can produce confident, wrong answers, and pretending otherwise would be dishonest. We reduce it by retrieving from primary sources before answering, by blocking the model from citing anything not in that retrieved material, and by telling it to admit a gap rather than fill it. It still won't be right every time. Treat it as a well-read analyst you argue with — not an oracle. Check anything you'd stake a decision or an exam answer on against the source.
