The COVID-19 pandemic caused the deepest global recession since the Great Depression, hitting nearly every economy at the same time.

Key facts (verified):
- The IMF estimated the world economy shrank by about 3.1–3.5% in 2020 — the worst peacetime contraction since the 1930s.
- The shock was synchronised: about 9 in 10 IMF member countries were expected to contract, so countries could not lean on trade or external demand to cushion the blow.
- 2020 GDP forecasts: US about -5.9%, EU about -7.1%, with Italy (about -9.1%) and Spain (about -8%) among the hardest hit in Europe.
- Cumulative 2020–2021 global output loss was estimated near US$9 trillion.
- Lasting "scarring" was expected via lower investment, lost schooling/human capital, and disrupted supply chains.

Why it matters as an analogue: the template for a synchronised, exogenous global demand-and-supply shock and the scale of the fiscal/monetary response — useful for analysing pandemic-style shocks, supply chains, and recovery scarring.
